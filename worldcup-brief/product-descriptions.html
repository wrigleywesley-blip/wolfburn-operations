10 Year Old · Scotland v Haiti.

The opening-match dram. Cream and gold. Sunday 14 June, Foxborough. The optimistic pour.
Product title~64 chars
Wolfburn 10 Year Old · World Cup Edition · Scotland v Haiti
Short description · for collection cards160 chars · 1-2 sentences
The opening dram. A 10 Year Old single malt Scotch whisky for Scotland's first World Cup match in twenty-eight years. Cream and gold. Hand-bottled in Thurso.
Full description · product page body~280 words

The 10 Year Old is the opening match of the set. We have given it warm cream and gold because openings are an inherently optimistic act, and we did not want to dress the first dram of the summer in anything that looked as if it knew the score in advance.

It is matured in second-fill Oloroso sherry hogsheads and ex-bourbon casks. The result is a whisky with the depth of character of something that has been here a while, and the temperament of one that does not feel the need to introduce itself loudly.

The label carries the date, the venue, the opponent, and a small Saltire raised against a darker flag. The lions are rampant on both sides. The pour is for Sunday 14 June, at nine o'clock in the evening BST, when Scotland walks out at Gillette Stadium in Foxborough and plays a World Cup match for the first time since 1998.

Pour it before the players come out. Sit it on the table. Let it breathe. Whichever way the result goes, the bottle has been here longer than the result.

Released individually, or as part of the Drams for the Tartan Army wooden set of three.

Mibbe aye, mibbe no. Fortune favours the brave.

Full description · product page body~290 words

The 8 Year Old is the second match of the set. The cobalt-blue label is the cooler colour, because by the time Scotland walks out for Morocco on Friday 19 June, the country has had five days to think about what happened on the Sunday. A cooler colour is what those five days call for.

It is the youngest in the Drams for the Tartan Army release. Brighter on the nose, more direct on the palate, with the kind of clarity a second-match dram needs. Pour it slowly. The fixture will reward composure.

The label carries the date, the venue, the opponent, and a small Saltire raised against a darker flag. The lions are rampant on both sides. The pour is for Friday 19 June, at eight o'clock in the evening BST, when Scotland returns to Gillette Stadium in Foxborough to play Morocco in the match that decides what happens against Brazil the following Wednesday.

Do not look at the form guide. The bottle will hold the composure for you.

Released individually, or as part of the Drams for the Tartan Army wooden set of three.

Product title~64 chars
Wolfburn 12 Year Old · World Cup Edition · Scotland v Brazil
Short description · for collection cards160 chars
The flagship. A 12 Year Old single malt for Scotland's group fixture against Brazil. Golden yellow. The deepest dram for the deepest moment.
Full description · product page body~310 words

The 12 Year Old is the third match of the set, and the flagship of the Wolfburn range. We have given it golden yellow because what the Brazil match asks for is gold, regardless of which way the result goes.

Two things to know about the match. The first is that the kick-off in Miami is at two o'clock in the morning BST, which means most of the country will either be awake when they should be asleep or asleep when they should be awake, and either choice is correct. The second is that Brazil have won the World Cup five times. Scotland have not. We can sit with that.

It is twelve years in oak. Matured in hand-selected Oloroso sherry butts and ex-bourbon casks. The result is a whisky with a lingering finish that does not require a particular result to justify itself.

The label carries the date, the venue, the opponent, and a small Saltire raised against a darker flag. The lions are rampant on both sides. The pour is for Wednesday 24 June, when Scotland plays Brazil at the Hard Rock Stadium and a country quietly holds its breath against all reasonable expectation.

Pour it slowly, somewhere between half past one and a quarter to two in the morning UK time. Sit it on the side table. Look at it once. Do not look at the form guide.

Released individually, or as part of the Drams for the Tartan Army wooden set of three.

Mibbe aye, mibbe no. Fortune favours the brave.

Product title~68 chars
Drams for the Tartan Army · Wolfburn World Cup Set of Three
Short description · for collection cards160 chars
A wooden box of three single malt Scotch whiskies, hand-bottled in Thurso for Scotland's three group matches at the 2026 World Cup. Limited release.
Full description · product page body~360 words

Drams for the Tartan Army is a Wolfburn release marking Scotland's first World Cup in twenty-eight years. Three single malt Scotch whiskies, hand-bottled in our distillery on the very north edge of Scotland, presented together in a wooden box.

Each bottle is tied to a specific match.

  • The 10 Year Old · cream and gold. For Scotland v Haiti. Gillette Stadium, Foxborough. Sunday 14 June, 21:00 BST.
  • The 8 Year Old · cobalt blue. For Scotland v Morocco. Gillette Stadium, Foxborough. Friday 19 June, 20:00 BST.
  • The 12 Year Old · golden yellow. For Scotland v Brazil. Hard Rock Stadium, Miami. Wednesday 24 June, 02:00 BST.

You receive the box. You open the bottles in the order the matches arrive. You pour them whichever way each result goes. The bottles do not require a particular outcome to justify themselves. They have been here longer than the result.

The wooden box is the kind a watchmaker might use to ship a serious thing. The interior is lined in a charcoal cloth that the Saltire would not be ashamed to be wrapped in. The three bottles sit in moulded recesses that hold them firmly enough that you could carry the box to Foxborough yourself, if you happened to be going.

It is a single limited release. When the box is gone, the box is gone. We will not repeat the set, for the simple reason that we will not repeat the summer.

First allocation to the newsletter list. Storefront open thereafter.

Mibbe aye, mibbe no. Fortune favours the brave.
